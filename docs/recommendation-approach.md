# Recommendation approach

Status: proposal. No engine, calibrated scores, or validated recommendation accuracy exists yet.

## Governing assessment principles

Follow assessment-principles.md: purpose, legibility, and readability are core; mood/harmony and hierarchy contribute supporting evidence. Preserve audience and context throughout. Keep evidence by area; do not collapse everything into one quality percentage. Source-tag matching alone cannot establish role suitability or reader performance.

## Separate filtering, ranking, and combination review

1. Filter for practical requirements: language, required styles, usage permissions, and availability. Unknown required data needs review; it should not silently pass.
2. Rank eligible families against project roles and visual preferences using attributes supported by evidence.
3. Assemble the requested family count with explicit roles.
4. Evaluate the complete combination: hierarchy, compatibility with the project, readable body treatment, and unnecessary competition.
5. Explain why each family was selected, the role it serves, and relevant tradeoffs.
6. Let people compare and revise results with their own content.

Do not rank families independently and assume the top 3 form a good combination.

## Before introducing scores

For each proposed dimension define:
- What the dimension measures.
- Whether it belongs to the family, a specific style, or a rendered context.
- Its endpoints or categories and contrasting examples.
- Accepted evidence and handling of disagreement.
- Unknown-data behavior.
- Whether it filters, ranks, or supports an explanation.
- Rubric version and review date.

For example, expressiveness could start as reviewed descriptive categories. It should not become a 1-5 score until the levels are meaningfully defined.

## Future score behavior

A transparent rule-based pilot is a proposal, not a stack decision. Keep required checks outside preference totals. Do not compensate for missing required characters with a strong mood match.

Missing judgments are not zero or midpoint values. Compare candidates with adequate comparable evidence, show evidence coverage, or retain them as unscored alternatives. Missing mood evidence alone must not eliminate a practically suitable family. Avoid percentages that imply scientific certainty.

User preference weights and confidence in research are different concepts. A confident description can still describe a poor match for this project.

## Pairing questions

Does each family have a distinct role? Are heading and paragraph treatments distinguishable? Does the combination support the surrounding design? Are character shapes, visual size, spacing, and weight relationships workable at the intended settings?

Useful contrast can come from size and weight within 1 family. Different categories do not guarantee a useful pairing. Additional families add complexity and may add loading cost.

## Test the system

Start with explicit example project briefs and a small reviewed font set. Inspect explanations and edge cases, then ask readers to compare actual previews. Test comprehension and suitability separately from personal liking.

Retain rejected combinations and short reasons when useful for improving rules. Do not treat a click or a selected result alone as proof that the recommendation was good.
