# First guided test

Status: ready as a proposed conversation exercise. No product interface, automatic recommendation engine, or participant results exist. The goal is to find unclear questions and missing design intentions before building a scoring system.

## What Johnny can try

Start with one real or fictional project. Answer these three prompts in your own words:

1. What are you making, who will read it, and what do they need to understand or do?
2. Do you want 1, 2, or 3 font families? Which parts need type, such as a heading, paragraph, button, or code sample?
3. What impression should it give, and what impression should it avoid? Use any words you want, even if they are absent from our inventory.

If useful, use this fictional brief: a community workshop page with a title, short explanation, schedule, and booking button. The reader needs to understand the activity and find the time and booking action. This is example content, not a claimed user requirement.

Ask only relevant follow-ups about language, required characters and styles, medium, reading length, existing design, or unclear impression words. Do not make people complete the whole attribute inventory.

## Compare actual samples

Use the [existing specimen gallery](../research/specimens/batch-1/README.md). Start with a heading or paragraph sheet appropriate to the task. Ask which samples seem worth exploring and what prompted that choice. Allow none, uncertainty, and a requested example outside the five-family pool.

The gallery is numbered and has a fixed order. It does not implement the balanced four-option schedule. Do not label this exploratory selection an attribute-comparison observation or insert it into that dataset. It is a conversation about preferences, including open comments and missing options.

Keep three answers distinct: what impression a sample suggests, whether the person likes it for this project, and whether they can use it comfortably for the task. Do not infer the first from the second. Character sheets alone do not establish character recognition.

## Discuss a provisional shortlist

After practical needs are checked, discuss the requested number of families with explicit roles and source-based reasons. Use the documented intended-use notes as attributed starting points and check the real content. Call the suggestions provisional; all five research records remain recommendation_eligible=false. A human-guided exploration is not an automatic engine result.

If the sample cannot meet the request, say so and retain the unmet requirement. Do not force a third family into a design without a job, or substitute a mood match for required characters. For a three-family request, ask what distinct task the third should serve. Pairing quality remains untested until the combination is actually shown and reviewed together.

The first session can stop after preferences if there is not enough evidence for a useful shortlist. Record what prevented the next step so a later batch addresses it. The small test should not wait for all fonts to be rated, but its five-family limit must stay clear.

## Capture findings without manufacturing scores

Record the project brief, verbatim impression words, requested family count and roles, clarified meanings, samples actually shown, stated preferences, missing examples, and unresolved requirements. Summarise back to Johnny before treating a phrase as interpreted correctly. Preserve disagreement; ask before quoting personal feedback publicly in the repository.

No participant feedback is currently stored. Do not prefill a completed session or interpret agreement with the project plan as evidence for particular font moods. Do not publish identifying project details by default.

## What a successful first test teaches

Can the person describe the goal without being forced into our vocabulary? Are the prompts understandable? Can we explain provisional choices and uncertainty? Which desired impression or role cannot be served by the sample? These observations guide the next iteration; they do not establish catalogue-wide fairness, reading performance, or recommendation accuracy.
