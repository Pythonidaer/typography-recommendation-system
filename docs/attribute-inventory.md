# Broader attribute inventory

Status: version 0.2 candidate inventory. No final vocabulary, numerical scale, or new font ratings are approved. This replaces the assumption that the original six words define the catalogue’s expressive range. Their historical definitions and records remain unchanged.

Johnny asked whether selecting only six words could hide suitable fonts. The earlier selection was influenced by convenient source tags and a small discussion workload; it was not established through catalogue-wide or user-needs validation. We will investigate a broader set before choosing assessment dimensions.

## Keep the questions separate

| Layer | What it asks | Proposed role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical requirements | Can this family support the required characters, styles, and use? | Confirmed requirements can filter; unknowns need review |
| Reading and text roles | How does this treatment work for the intended reading task? | Core evidence; test actual content and settings |
| Mood | What feeling does the sample suggest? | Optional preference ranking with adequate evidence |
| Social impression | What character or manner does it appear to project? | Optional association, never proof of honesty or competence |
| Formality | How ceremonial, relaxed, or refined does it appear? | Optional association; overlapping concepts need review |
| Presence | How understated or attention-seeking does it appear? | Context-dependent preference; consider size and layout |
| Period association | What historical or imagined period does it recall? | Style preference; ask for a more specific reference |
| Appearance | What do its visible forms suggest? | Descriptive preference; do not infer reading quality |

These groups organise research, not mutually exclusive scientific categories. A term may belong to more than one group. The reading requirements remain central across all of them.

## Preserve every existing expressive source label

All 20 expressive labels in the pinned source data are retained independently, including labels without a proposed candidate mapping. For example, Artistic, Awkward, Business, Childlike, Cute, Innovative, and Stiff are not lost because they do not fit neatly into the original six moods. They may need different wording or a different layer.

Do not use a source label such as Competent to imply technical quality, or Childlike to infer suitability for children. Historical and social associations depend on the sample, audience, language, and context.

## How to reduce the inventory

1. Start with open wording: ask what impression someone wants, without showing the list first. Preserve phrases that have no match.
2. Compare their wording with the candidate meanings. Ask what they mean by ambiguous terms before assigning a label.
3. Test whether people can distinguish near neighbours: Playful/Cheerful/Energetic, Friendly/Sincere, Formal/Elegant, and Restrained/Neutral/Calm.
4. Use the five existing rendered instances for a small preparation exercise. Do not treat them as validated high/low examples or catalogue representatives.
5. Keep, reword, merge, split, or defer terms based on usefulness, comprehension, and evidence. Record the reason and definition version.

Do not force independent terms into opposite pairs. Do not delete a useful design goal merely because the provider has no convenient tag. If a request remains unmapped, retain its original wording and use visual examples or a clarification rather than silently choosing a nearest label.

## What sufficient coverage would require

Source availability, understandable vocabulary, useful distinctions, and recommendation performance are different checks. Record which supported design goals lack evidence. Evaluate missed suitable candidates as well as chosen results. Inspect results by provider category and script using a defined catalogue snapshot; the current five-family sample cannot answer those questions.

There is no evidence-based fixed number of dimensions yet. Keep the assessment short through conditional questions, while maintaining a broader internal inventory. Mood associations should help order otherwise suitable choices; an absent mood tag must not automatically eliminate a family.
