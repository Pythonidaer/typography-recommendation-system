# Project questions: draft 0.1

Status: wording and branching proposal. No interface or participant responses exist. The agreed principles in assessment-principles.md govern this draft.

## Keep the first pass short

Group questions into 3 stages. Show follow-ups only when an answer changes the next decision. Let people say “Not sure” and revise answers. Unknowns remain visible; they are not automatically filled with assumptions.

The wording below is an initial question bank, not a requirement to show every prompt to every visitor.

## 1. Describe the project

| ID | Proposed prompt | Answer format | Why we ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| project_goal | What should someone understand or do after reading? | Short text; optional example: “Find an event time and reserve a place” | Connect recommendations to purpose |
| medium | Where will the typography appear? | Website / app / printed document / poster or sign / other / not sure | Establish the medium and conditions |
| reading_tasks | How will people use the text? | Select all: scan short labels, find information, read paragraphs, read long passages, read code or data, not sure | Identify the reading task and likely roles |

Medium follow-up: ask about screen/device or viewing distance only when relevant. The pilot evidence is for selected Google Fonts files, not proof that every medium or device works.

## 2. Set practical needs

| ID | Proposed prompt | Answer format | Why we ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| audience_needs | What do you know about the people reading it? | Optional short text with examples: language, reading experience, need for larger text, device, lighting; include “Not sure yet” | Establish audience context without inferring ability from age |
| language_characters | Which languages and characters does the text need? | Language list and optional actual text or symbols; “Not sure” is explicit | Check support; do not infer completeness from a subset name |
| family_count | How many font families would you like to use? | 1 / 2 / 3 | Fulfill the requested family count |

Helper for family count: “A family can include regular, bold, and italic styles.”

Role follow-up: “Which parts of the design need a text style?” Offer headings, paragraphs, navigation/buttons, labels/captions, code/data, and other. Preselect nothing unless supplied by the person. Suggest roles from reading tasks only as editable proposals.

If 3 families are selected, ask “What would you like the third family to do?” A defined role is needed before presenting a complete 3-family recommendation. Explain the missing role rather than silently returning 2 families.

Optional styles follow-up: “Do you need particular styles, such as bold or italic?” Answers are named styles, a specific requirement, or not sure. Treat stated requirements as checks; leave later weight experimentation available.

## 3. Describe the intended appearance

| ID | Proposed prompt | Answer format | Why we ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| existing_design | What does the typography need to work with? | Optional palette, images, existing fonts, references, or description; “Starting fresh” | Evaluate harmony in context |
| desired_character | How would you like the design to feel? | Own words initially; optional examples only after mood vocabulary is reviewed | Record desired character without asserting a font has it |
| importance | Which needs matter most for this project? | Name a priority and an optional must-have; no numeric weights yet | Separate requirements from preference importance |

Mood examples currently remain proposals in mood-vocabulary.md. Keep vintage/futuristic style and business context available separately. Do not force people to choose from the 6 draft mood words.

Later optional visual preference questions ask which sample suits their project. Research attribute questions ask which sample expresses a named mood; do not combine their response fields.

## What answers can change

- Purpose and reading task inform proposed text roles and candidate explanations.
- Required characters or styles trigger verification. A known failure blocks that candidate for that requirement.
- Family count constrains the output, with roles for each family.
- Unknown technical requirements keep the recommendation provisional; they do not count as verified.
- Mood and references guide preference exploration, subject to evidence gaps.
- Aesthetic preferences cannot compensate for known missing required characters.

No score formula or arbitrary numeric mapping is introduced.

## Example brief: research fixture only

Goal: find workshop details and reserve a place.
Medium: website, phone and desktop.
Reading task: scan for time and location, then read a short explanation.
Audience: adult readers of English; other needs not yet known.
Characters: actual sample text plus labels and punctuation; validate those exact strings.
Family count: 2.
Roles: heading; paragraph and interface labels.
Existing design: no image reference or palette yet. Contextual harmony remains untested.
Desired character: not chosen.
Styles: normal and a possible stronger heading; specific weight not required yet.

This is fictional test input, not Johnny’s answers or evidence about the audience. Missing mood does not prevent independent character or role checks. Missing reading evidence must stay visible.

## Review questions for the question bank

Can someone explain what each prompt asks? Can they answer without knowing typography terms? Are follow-ups relevant? Can they distinguish a requirement from a preference? Do they understand what “Not sure” leaves unverified?

Use observed confusion to revise wording before committing to a fixed assessment length.
