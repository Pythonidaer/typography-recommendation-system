How the fields and points work

Category: Google Fonts’ broad classification. Monospace is a spacing property; it can overlap serif or sans serif. The equal-width check compares the supplied file’s A–Z and a–z advances.

Associations: source-linked descriptions such as print, digital, handwriting, or typewriter. These are separate from mood.

Roles: heading and accent roles are proposals to compare. The supporting-text shortlist uses reviewed text or interface descriptions. Noto Sans JP is a separate editorial proposal based on script coverage, marked in the table. “Unknown” means evidence is still missing; it does not mean a font is unsuitable.

Rule points: transparent, provisional sorting rules for the brief shown below. These are not percentages or measured font attributes. H = heading, B = body, A = accent. A dash means the font fails that role’s requirements.

Membership in the supporting-text shortlist adds 12 points to body/all-text roles. The chosen heading type adds 30 to a heading/all-text role. A matching lettering association adds 20 to display roles, or 6 to body/all-text roles. Retro influence adds 2 per slider step to documented print, digital, retro, or typewriter associations. Rough influence adds 2 per step to headings, or 5 to accents, for documented rough associations. A contrasting accent adds 20 to a rough candidate only when rough influence is above zero; it skips the main association bonus.

Not measured: mood, reading performance, and pairing scores remain unknown for every family. The reading-length answer and written goal provide context for evaluation; they are not semantic matching inputs.
